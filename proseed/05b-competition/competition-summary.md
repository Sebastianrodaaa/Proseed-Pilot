# Step 5b — Competition Summary: H7

## The map

Full table in `competition-map.csv`. Three tiers: incumbent comminution OEMs (Metso, FLSmidth, Weir ~60–70% of large mills), mid-tier HPGR specialists, and a thin emerging pre-treatment niche (SELFRAG, MicroHammer).

## Where the gaps are

1. **Nobody productizes continuous pre-treatment.** The white space is real: OEMs sell incremental efficiency inside the mill; pre-treatment players are either batch/lab-scale (SELFRAG economics don't survive 1,000 t/h) or unproductized (MicroHammer is a spin-off with a demo, not an install base).
2. **The retrofit slot is unclaimed.** Every competitor either replaces circuit equipment (HPGR, new line) or adds a different unit op (ore sorting). A bolt-on module ahead of the existing mill fights nobody's installed product directly — which is also why it channels well through an OEM partner.
3. **The real competitor is "do nothing."** Discovery showed the default choice is another year of blast tuning. The pitch isn't "better than X" — it's "step-change vs. the single-digit ceiling you're stuck at."

## Threat assessment

| Threat | Severity | Mitigation |
|--------|----------|------------|
| OEM acqui-kill (Weir/FLSmidth partner-then-shelve) | High — it's how the industry absorbs threats | Step 11: patent the applicator + control method before partner talks; keep ore-database as trade secret |
| MicroHammer reaches market first with weaker product | Medium — could poison the category with a bad first install | Move fast on a credible pilot; differentiate on closed-loop dose control (their public gap) |
| "Free" in-house tuning absorbs demand | Low — ceiling is known and low | Sell against the ceiling, not against tuning |

## What this means for positioning

Position as **the retrofit debottlenecking step the OEMs don't sell** — credible enough that an OEM partnership is their cheapest entry (channel) and our biggest IP risk (Step 11 must run before any partner demo).
