# Step 9a — Market Traction (Commercial)

## Purpose

Find out whether the market will commit if ProSeed delivers. Part 1 built the value proposition from evidence; this step tests it against the only court that matters — customers deciding whether to engage.

## Inputs

- `proseed/08-value-proposition/value-proposition-onepager.md` — the page you now take to market
- `proseed/03-customer-discovery/` — the interested customers and LOIs already collected
- `proseed/06-customer-requirements/customer-requirements.md` — the buying process map

## Method

1. **Sell to clients** with the one-pager. Not pitching a vision — asking for the commitment the document supports.
2. **Set up a CRM and sales funnel.** Every prospect, every stage, every next action. The funnel is the deliverable's skeleton: conversion at each stage is the traction metric.
3. **Identify the most interested customers** — segment them by enthusiasm AND by how representative they are. Early enthusiasts who are atypical can mislead.
4. **Collect letters of interest and commitments.** Rank them: verbal interest < written LOI < conditional commitment < paid commitment. The gate in the next step weighs these differently.
5. **Record objections as data.** Every "not now", "too expensive", "missing spec X" goes into the funnel notes — they feed Step 13 pricing and Step 16 feedback.

## Deliverable

`proseed/09a-market-traction/` — the sales funnel export or summary (`traction-report.md`): prospects by stage, LOIs and commitments collected (with the documents), objection log, and the most-interested-customer list. If `templates/` contains templates, use them.

Update `proseed/STATUS.md`.

## Red Flags

| Thought | Reality |
|---------|---------|
| "Lots of interest out there" | Interest in a spreadsheet is traction. Interest in the air is not. |
| "They loved the one-pager" | Loved is not committed. Push for the letter or the signature. |
| "Objections are rejections" | Objections are requirements you hadn't priced. Log them. |
| "The first enthusiast is the beachhead" | Check they're representative before designing for them. |
