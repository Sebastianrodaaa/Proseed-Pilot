# Step 10b — Paid Trials (Commercial)

## Purpose

Customers pay to test the product — and in markets where customers buy through pilots, this step IS the sales engine, not a side activity. A paying tester is the strongest commitment signal short of a contract, and their money buys you honest feedback instead of politeness.

## Inputs

- `proseed/decision-gate.md` — must say the trial route is on, and why
- `proseed/09a-market-traction/` — the most interested customers
- `proseed/05a-feasibility/feasibility-report.md` — the results trials should be in line with

## Method

1. **Price the trial deliberately.** What customers pay for a trial sets the anchor for the real price — free trials also attract tourists. Decide what the trial fee covers.
2. **Define success before the trial starts:** the conditions, the metrics, the duration, and what result counts as validated vs. failed. Written down, agreed with the customer.
3. **Run and document:** conditions, setup, raw measurements, deviations, what the customer did with the product that you didn't expect.
4. **Compare with feasibility.** Results should be in line with Step 5a. Divergence is the finding — investigate whether it's the product, the setting, or the customer's process.
5. **Capture the commercial learnings:** what they'd pay for real, what would make them sign, which objections surfaced under real use.

## Deliverable

`proseed/10b-paid-trials/` — per-trial reports (agreement, conditions, measurements, outcome vs. success criteria, customer statements) plus a `trials-synthesis.md`. If `templates/` contains templates, use them.

Update `proseed/STATUS.md`.

## Red Flags

| Thought | Reality |
|---------|---------|
| "A free trial is easier to sell" | Easy yes, weak signal. The payment IS the validation. |
| "We'll see how the trial goes" | Success criteria written before, or the trial teaches nothing. |
| "The trial failed, bad luck" | A failed trial at 10b is cheap learning. Investigate, don't shrug. |
| "Results differed from the lab, ignore it" | Divergence from 5a is the single most interesting finding. Chase it. |
