# ProSeed --- Hypothesis Validation Process

The process contains 16 steps across two parts:

-   **Part 1 --- Find the value proposition:** Steps 1--8.
-   **Part 2 --- Sell, build and learn:** Steps 9--16.

The workflow branches between Commercial and Technical work, with joint
steps, decision points, and iteration loops.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Step descriptions

1.  **Problem hypotheses --- Together**
    -   Identify which customers have a problem worth solving and what
        they are trying to achieve.
    -   Formulate: "We believe \[customers\] want \[outcome\] because
        \[problem\]."
    -   Start from the customer's goal, not from a proposed solution.
2.  **Market scan --- Commercial**
    -   Identify target customers and estimate how many there are.
    -   Research who serves the need today, market size, and regulation.
    -   Method: desk research per hypothesis; produce a sourced
        document.
3.  **Tech baseline --- Technical**
    -   Understand how customers solve the problem today and which
        technologies they use.
    -   Assess current performance, limitations, and where microwaves
        could do better.
    -   Method: desk research and ProSeed trial data; produce a sourced
        comparison table.
4.  **Customer discovery --- Commercial**
    -   Test whether the problem is real and important.
    -   Understand what customers use today, where it falls short, who
        decides, and who pays.
    -   Interview end users; technical team joins half the interviews.
        Ask about their process, not ProSeed's idea. Collect letters of
        interest and centralize transcripts.
5.  **Concept --- Technical**
    -   Explore what the solution could look like and which alternatives
        it would compete with.
    -   Produce a one-page concept based on discovery. The solution
        could be a machine, software, service, or a mix.

5a. **Feasibility --- Technical** - Determine whether the proposed
solution works. - Validate the technology with a proof of concept; where
applicable, include lab test reports, pictures, software, or drawings.

5b. **Competition --- Commercial** - Identify competing players, product
types, specifications, and business models. - Conduct in-depth, sourced
research, supported by people in the field. Map findings in Excel and
summarize gaps where needed.

6.  **Customer requirements and specifications --- Commercial**
    -   Define the conditions the solution must satisfy and the
        properties customers expect.
    -   Understand how customers buy, who decides, and the buying
        timeline.
    -   Discuss with buyers and decision-makers; compare requirements
        with competing solutions.

7a. **Concept, requirement and specification assumptions ---
Technical** - Define concept assumptions, requirements and
specifications ProSeed meets today or could reach. - Build a first
roadmap, estimate when targets could be reached, and identify potential
IP. - Use desk research and tests, with expert support. Produce a 1--2
page concept and first roadmap.

7b. **Unit economics --- Commercial** - Assess whether unit economics
work for the final product compared with the best existing solutions. -
Build an Excel model and iterate with the technical team on Step 7a.

8.  **Value proposition --- Together**
    -   Consolidate what the product is, the gain for the client, key
        requirements and specifications, and how long ProSeed can stay
        ahead in the market.
    -   Output: a commercial one-pager, ready to support selling.

## Step descriptions

9a. **Market traction --- Commercial** - Assess how the market reacts
and whether customers would commit if ProSeed delivers. - Identify the
most interested customers. - Sell to clients, set up a CRM and sales
funnel, and collect letters of interest and commitments.

9b. **Concept, requirement and specification validation ---
Technical** - Validate technically the requirements and
specifications. - Produce the technical specification (*cahier des
charges*) and a roadmap covering budget, resources, timeline, and
milestones. - Run workshops with experts and companies.

**Decision --- Move forward?** - Assess whether market traction
justifies the spending and whether requirements and specifications are
already met. - Review letters of interest, validated roadmap, and
budget. - Choose among design and engineering, optional paid trials, or
product concept design. - If requirements are already met, the process
can move directly to Step 12.

10a. **Requirements and specifications design and engineering ---
Technical** - Launch the validated roadmap. - Design and engineer the
requirements and specifications. - Apply the roadmap and validate
requirements in design and concept.

10b. **Paid trials --- Commercial (optional)** - Customers pay to test
the product. - Run and document trials; results should be in line with
feasibility.

11. **IP strategy --- Technical**

-   Validate the IP mix and determine what must be protected before it
    is shown.
-   Work with experts on patents and documented trade secrets.

12. **Product concept design --- Technical**

-   Complete the design of the commercial product or pilot.
-   Establish exact costs, installation and support approach, and design
    readiness.
-   Produce the technical specification and report.

13. **Pricing and model --- Commercial**

-   Validate the final business model and price, including margin on
    real costs.
-   Run unit economics and assess financial needs.

14. **Market validation --- Commercial**

-   Determine whether customers will sign the final offer.
-   Finalize and convert the sales funnel; secure contracts.

15. **Product development --- Technical**

-   Develop the pilot unit, MVP, or first product.
-   Apply the IP decision.
-   Output: a working version installed and running.

16. **Customer feedback, next version --- Together**

-   Understand what works, what does not, what must improve, and which
    new requirements and specifications are needed.
-   Test the product, discuss it with users, and validate unit
    economics.
-   Feed new requirements into design and engineering for Version 2
    (Step 10a).

## Ownership by step

  Ownership        Steps
  ---------------- -------------------------------------------
  **Commercial**   2a, 3, 5b, 6, 7b, 9a, 10b, 13, 14
  **Technical**    2b, 4, 5a, 7a, 9b, 10a, 11, 12, 15
  **Together**     1, 8, 16
  **Decision**     Move forward? (between Steps 9 and 10/12)

## Workflow characteristics

-   **Parallel work:** Steps 2a and 2b are parallel commercial and
    technical investigations.
-   **Iteration:** Steps 7a and 7b iterate with each other.
-   **Decision gate:** After Steps 9a and 9b, ProSeed decides whether to
    move forward and which route to take.
-   **Optional activity:** Step 10b (paid trials) is optional.
-   **Version loop:** Feedback at Step 16 generates new requirements
    that return to Step 10a for Version 2.
