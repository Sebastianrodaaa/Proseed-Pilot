## Verdict on the hypothesis

**Supported — with one significant reframe.**

The problem is real, quantified, and publicly admitted by the customers themselves (energy reports, sustainability disclosures, conference talks). The reframe: the buying wedge is **brownfield debottlenecking**, not new-plant circuits. Every evidence line — EPCM conservatism, "be second not first" culture, permitting costs of new lines — points the same way.

## What discovery changed

1. **The ask shifted from energy to throughput.** Miners value −15% kWh/t, but they *pay for* +20–30% throughput on a bottlenecked SAG line — the same physics, priced differently. This feeds straight into Step 7b's value model.
2. **Ore-specificity is the credibility gate.** "Show me on MY ore" is the recurring objection — a paid bench/pilot test on the customer's own ore is the expected proof step (relevant to Step 10b design).
3. **Decarbonization is a second buyer.** EU/corporate CO₂ reporting makes grinding kWh a board metric; the sustainability committee is a second route into the account.
4. **OEM reaction is partner-shaped, not enemy-shaped** — but carries acqui-kill risk → feeds Step 11 IP strategy.

## Which hypotheses this touches

H7 stands. H8 (yields) gets a tailwind — same buyers, same ore-specificity requirement; keep H7 primary, H8 as the upsell once liberation data exists.

## Buying map (from public evidence + interviews)

Initiator: site GM or processing superintendent → Validator: corporate metallurgy/innovation committee → Signer: capex committee (>$5M) → Blocker: plant manager whose uptime the trial disrupts. Cycle: 24–36 months plant-modifying; 12–18 months for mid-tier single-site.
